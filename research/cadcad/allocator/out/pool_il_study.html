How IL works, in dollars

Impermanent loss is a ratio, so it is easy to quote and hard to feel. Here it is a dollar loss against holding the same entry basket.

Static IL in USD - $10,000 at NVDA $225.80

Ranges ±5%, ±10%, ±20% - price moves -20% to +20%

Every line is zero at entry and below zero on both sides. The narrower the range, the steeper the loss for the same price move.

IL = LP value - HODL value for the same entry basket, in USD on a $10,000 position. Static range, no fees, no rebalancing. Negative means the LP is behind the unmanaged basket.

IL is the price of being useful. At a 20% fall the IL on a ±5% range is -$901.50 on $10,000 versus holding; on a ±20% range it is -$618.31. At a 20% rise the ±5% range IL is -$876.23. Narrower ranges earn more fees and lose more to IL on the same move, so fees must cover IL before the book beats HODL. The guard and recall protect the tail, not this trade-off: they can take you out of the book, but they do not make a narrow range cheap. The model's LP mark versus HODL can still be positive in a down tape because the book is underweight NVDA and sits out part of the fall; that is a timing and allocation effect, not free alpha.

How we leave

  1. Risk HIGH. Depeg, guard absent, or a volatility and volume blowout: stop adding and let the keeper act.
  2. Guard pull (designed first line). The 200 bps five-minute proxy is meant to pull the guard before the whole book is closed. It is a proxy, not a live auction; in this run it did not fire, so the recall below did the work.
  3. Allocator recall second. Capital leaves the book for the arm or idle when the book is not worth the IL. In this tape that is 2 recalls.
  4. Re-arm when calm. The keeper re-enters when risk normalizes; a recall is not an exit from the strategy.

Guard share 75 means the guard holds 75% of book capital, not a 75 bps width.

Protection is conditional. The guard is designed as the first line and the allocator recall as the second. In this run it did not pull (guard_pulls = 0), so the allocator recall did the work: 2 recalls in risk-only A. The mode strip and allocation stack show capital leaving the book. Over 45 days the strategy drawdown is -5.19% versus HODL -6.66%, but over 20 days it is -7.65% versus -6.53%. The $15k floor improves the 20-day cut to -6.62% and the focus case to -6.65%, still not better than HODL in that window. Do not claim unconditional protection.
08

Takeaways

Five things to carry into the next review, with the caveat attached.

  • Pool returns. Fee APR is not return. The best USDG net APR is 14.6% (USDG/NVDA V4 0.30%: 22.4% fees minus 7.8% IL), but total APR is -11.1% in a falling tape. The ETH book's 38.7% net is in WETH.
  • Morpho and the arm. Arm carry is $1.26 in risk-only A and $15.11 in always-arms over 45 days: a safe harbor, not the engine. The 107.29% borrow stress is a liquidity and exit trap, not free yield.
  • Strategy cases. A 2h/15k/75 nets +$431.36 / +$46.95. The base risk-only A nets +$191.04 / -$105.62; the $15k floor flips the 20-day cut.
  • IL. A $10,000 position in a ±5% range carries -$901.50 of IL at a 20% fall and -$876.23 at a 20% rise versus holding. Wider ranges carry less; narrower ranges earn more fees and lose more to IL.
  • Protection. The guard is designed as the first line, the recall as the second; in this tape the recall did the work. The 45d drawdown is -5.19% versus HODL -6.66%, but the 20d cut is -7.65% versus -6.53%: protection is conditional.
Not in this tape

A 45-minute keeper, no volume floor, a 24-hour recall, and a low-fee exit variant are not in this tape. Re-run the model on fresh tape before sizing any of them. The numbers here are decision support from one down regime, not a track record.
