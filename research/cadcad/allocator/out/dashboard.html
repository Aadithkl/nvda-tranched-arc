Answers

Will it work on the real tape?

Yes on the book, no on the allocator alpha. The book earns real fees ($245–$278 over 45d), but the 20d NVDA USD fall overwhelms them. The as-built allocator is a one-way door into the arms (−$4.52 45d, +$4.98 20d, essentially always-arms). The coherent risk-only policy is +$191.04 45d / −$105.62 20d at 1h/10k/50%. The in-sample best is +$512.06 45d / +$81.77 20d at 3k base / 500 guard / 2.5k skew. But the 20-path bootstrap says C's median is close to always-A while its p05 is much worse (−$607 / −$159 vs −$507 / −$85). The in-sample gain is a tail-risk trade, not proven alpha.

Will the guard prevent IL?

No. The guard concentrates fees; it does not stop base IL. Guard share 0% / 50% / 75%: 45d +$44.46 / +$191.04 / +$273.69, 20d −$13.90 / −$105.62 / −$127.13. More guard = more fees in the 45d up/fee regime, worse 20d in the down move. The 75% guard only becomes positive in the 20d when combined with the 2h/15k filters (+$46.95). The ETH books are in range only 66–75% vs 91–92% for USDG, yet their IL is smaller because the ETH-quoted NVDA move is smaller (−3.0% vs −6.3%).

Is the APR justifiable? What should we expect?

The fee APR is real: USDG books gross 19.9–22.4%, IL −7.8 to −8.0%, net vs HODL 11.9–14.6%; after the NVDA USD price fall the total is −1.4 to −1.7% over 45d (−11 to −14% annualized). The ETH books gross 30.3–40.4%, IL −1.7 to −1.8%, net 28.5–38.7% in ETH, total +2.0 to +3.3% in ETH (16–26% annualized). The expected net vs HODL is the defensible LP number; the total is quote-price dependent. Do not quote the gross fee APR as a total return.

Does the 1-hour check stand?

The 1h check is defensible, but not optimal on this tape. 2h gives +$325.65 45d / +$29.60 20d vs +$191.04 / −$105.62 at 1h, with 167/160 actions vs 276/229. 4h and 8h are too slow for the 20d. The 2h/15k/75% combo is the in-sample best. Test 2h before changing production; the guard still rechecks every 5 minutes.

3% NVDA from Spiral, 30% vs 35% LTV

The 3% is the Spiral total yield on NVDA collateral, APR after borrowing cost, not a native Morpho collateral yield. The model grosses it up by LTV×borrow APR and subtracts borrow, so the net NVDA contribution is 3% APR. In the full always-arms loop the grossed-up NVDA line is $9.88 over 45d, alongside $7.54 USDG interest, $2.31 borrow and $1.68 gas → $13.43 net carry; the collateral mark takes it to +$12.87. The risk-only allocator holds the collateral only briefly; its NVDA contribution is $0.15. 35% LTV lowers the full-arm net to +$12.82 (30%: +$12.87) and drifts max LTV to 40.1% vs 34.4%. The extra 5% debt is borrowed at 3% and the lend cap is full; it does not pay. Keep the 29.375%/30% target.

How much arm + book rewards?

Book fees are the engine. In the in-sample best C over 45d: $338.07 book A fees + $1.54 arm USDG + $0.11 NVDA, less $4.18 borrow/gas/swap → $335.54 net carry ($27.2% APR). The LP mark/timing adds $176.53, so net vs HODL is $512.06. The arm is only $1.65 of that; the book is 99%+ of carry. The 3% NVDA is APR net of borrowing cost: the model grosses up the NVDA yield and then subtracts borrow. In the full always-arms loop the arm earns $17.42 gross but only +$12.87 after borrow/gas and the collateral mark. Book fee APR is $27.4% over 45d.

Does inventory / ratio management help?

It helps in-sample, not risk-adjusted. After a recall the book re-funds only $6.1k–$6.8k of ~$10k because the recalled token ratio no longer matches the LP. Swap-to-fund deploys the idle cash but fees rise $188 → $275 and net falls $191 → $187: the extra capital buys IL. Inventory skew (shift the base to use the recalled token, no swap) raises C's fees to $338 and net to $512 / $82. A direct no-skew 50/50 re-add + surplus NVDA arm sweep is +$497 / +$51: the reserve/50-50 part works, but pushing excess NVDA to the arm does not pay at $10k because the 20% arm cap is full and the 3% net yield is too small. The skew's bootstrap p05 is −$607 / −$159 vs baseline −$473 / −$78 and always-A −$507 / −$85. The price depreciation is now concentrated in the vault's NVDA inventory; that is exactly the tail risk.

Can fee > IL be improved?

The binary fee > IL check is not the binding constraint on this tape; the 5-minute guard never pulls at 200 bps. A fee/IL hurdle with a 24h window, 250 bps buffer and 8h confirmation improves the 1h baseline from +$191 / −$106 to +$302 / +$19 and cuts drawdown to 4.4%/6.7%, but it does not beat the 2h/15k/75 combo. Pulling the guard on a guard-specific fee/IL signal is worse (−$9 / −$31): it churns 136–225 pulls and collapses fees to $50–60. The better equation is a hurdle plus hysteresis plus gas, not a raw fee > IL trigger. The biggest fee lever is width/skew: base 3,000 and guard 500 raise fees to $338 over 45d, with the tail-risk caveat above.

ETH/NVDA vs USDG/NVDA

Same NVDA, different quote. ETH/NVDA fell −3.0% in ETH vs −6.3% in USDG over the window. The ETH book's total is +2.0 to +3.3% in ETH; the USDG book's total is −1.4 to −1.7% in USDG. The ETH book wins in ETH unless ETH/USD falls more than the breakeven (about −4.8% for the V3 0.05 pair). Use the slider to translate the ETH books into USD; the USDG books are already USD-like.

Does it scale to $1M / $2M?

Yes for Book A, no for Book B. With exact fee shares, C over 45d is +$40,164 at $1M ($32.6% net APR, book fee $16.6%) and +$76,349 at $2M ($31.0%, book fee $15.0%). But the 20d is −$21,155 at $1M and −$44,405 at $2M: the $10k 20d +$81.77 does not scale. Always-A is +$11,742 at $1M and +$19,036 at $2M, but Book B is only +$2,473 at $1M and −$3,726 at $2M; Book B becomes 21%/43% of its pool. The 20/50/100% arm caps give nearly the same 1M/2M C result; the arm is under $1k, so the book/idle/timing dominates. Do not linearly extrapolate $10k; size Book B at ~$250k–$500k and Book A at ~$1M–$2M only with a fresh-tape/tail test.

How to read this

Fee APR = fees / initial capital, annualized. IL APR = (LP value − HODL value) / initial capital, annualized. Net APR = fee + IL (the LP skill). HODL/price APR = the initial token basket's quote price move. Total APR = net + HODL/price. For the allocator P&L the identity is net = book fees + arm USDG + NVDA yield − borrow − gas − swap − liquidation + LP mark/timing. The “APR + price − IL” identity is: total = fee + IL + HODL, with IL negative. The USDG total is negative in this down window even though the net is positive; the ETH book's total is positive in ETH because the ETH-quoted NVDA fall is smaller and the fees are higher.

Caveats

Book A is a V3 0.05% proxy; no V4 USDG/NVDA 0.05% pool exists in the downloaded set. Book B is the real V4 0.30% pool. Both windows are down regimes; the block bootstrap preserves the same regime and does not prove a bull market. The in-sample best has a worse bootstrap p05 than always-A: it is a tail-risk trade, not free alpha. Guard pulls are a 5-minute proxy, not the hook EV/oracle recheck. The guard-specific fee/IL pull is not supported by this tape. Arm rates are scenarios, not a 45-day Morpho history. The 3% NVDA is a user/Spiral assumption, not a native collateral yield. ETH/USD is not in the swap tapes; the slider is an explicit translation, not a historical series. Swap cost is 10 bps in the swap-to-fund case and zero otherwise; no MEV/slippage/price impact. The re-center action is modelled as one composite action with 2× gas; the keeper would need two runs or a composite adapter. Gas is $0.02/action. The model assumes the allocator executes and applies module caps, not controller timelocks/caps.
