Should we split between the 0.05 and 0.30 pools?

Two sleeves, same $100,000, same 45-day tape. The 0.05 sleeve and the 0.30 sleeve run at the same time, each with the recommended 75% guard / 25% base shape. Fees use the exact share L / (pool L + L), so moving more into the thin pool dilutes your own fee share. No re-centering and no inventory skew: this isolates the pool-choice effect.

Pool-only, a partial sleeve beats both 100% 0.05 and 100% 0.30. On the actual tape the best pool-only split is about 43% in the 0.30 pool, worth +$189 over 45 days versus all-0.05. But against our actual risk-only strategy, the sleeve is negative at 1×. It turns positive at 2× over 45 days (3× over the last 20) and adds about +$10,064 at 10× with a 28% sleeve. Moving 100% into the 0.30 pool is not “owning 100%” — it makes you about 15.6% of that pool.

4. The dynamic rule

Move into the 0.30 pool until its marginal fee dollar equals the 0.05 sleeve's fee dollar. The marginal yield falls as your share rises, so the optimum is finite:

s1 = your fee share if 100% were in 0.30
a = s1 / (1 - s1)
w* = ( sqrt(F_B * a / F_A) - 1 ) / a
w = min(w*, w_cap), w_cap = cap / (a * (1 - cap))

F_B = 0.30 pool fee revenue over the horizon
F_A = your 0.05 fee revenue at full capital
cap = your risk limit on owning the 0.30 pool

6. What this does and does not prove

The 10× volume is a sensitivity, not a forecast. It holds the 0.30 pool's liquidity fixed while multiplying its volume 10×. In reality that volume would attract more liquidity and dilute the fee APR. The sleeves are also passive: no re-centering, no inventory skew, and no Morpho overlay on the 0.30 side. The 20-day column is the tail of the same tape, not independent evidence. The result that survives all of these caveats is the shape: pool-only, a partial sleeve beats both extremes at 1×; against the risk-only engine, the sleeve only pays at 2× or more; and the cap — not the fee math — is what limits the 10× case.
