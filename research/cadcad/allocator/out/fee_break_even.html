3. The check and move function

rho = F_B_pool / F_A_pool (the visible fee ratio)
r = F_B / F_A = rho / share_A
s1 = your fee share if 100% were in 0.30
a = s1 / (1 - s1)

Delta(w) = m * F_B * s(w) - w * (F_A + H)
s(w) = w * a / (1 + w * a)

w* = ( sqrt( m * F_B * a / (F_A + H) ) - 1 ) / a
w = clamp(w*, 0, w_cap), w_cap = cap / (a * (1 - cap))

F_B = 0.30 pool fee revenue over the horizon
F_A = your 0.05 fee revenue at full capital
H = risk-only net minus all-0.05 net (the return you give up by moving)
cap = your limit on the 0.30 share

This is the whole rule. H is the “further loss” term: moving capital out of the risk-only engine costs w × H, so the fee gain must clear it. If you compare only with passive all-0.05, set H = 0; the same formula gives the pool-only optimum. At current fees the risk-only version gives w* = 0, while the fee-only version gives 46%. At 1.5× the risk-only version gives about 51%, capped at 28%; at 2× it wants 100%, capped at 28%.

5. The historical fee signal

Seven-day rolling fee ratio over the 45-day tape: min 4.51%, median 8.06%, max 23.60%. At 1.5× those become 6.76% / 12.09% / 35.39%. The 28% risk-only threshold is 9.99%; the signal crosses it in the better weeks and falls below it in the weaker weeks. That is why the keeper needs a rolling window and hysteresis, not a single 45-day average.

6. What this does and does not prove

The fee ratio is historical, not a forecast. The volume multiples scale the 0.30 fee counter while holding liquidity fixed; real volume growth attracts liquidity and dilutes the fee APR. The sleeves are passive: no re-centering, no skew, no Morpho overlay on the 0.30 side. The risk-only comparison is an overlay approximation. The result that survives is the function and the break-even levels: the 0.30 pool is already fee-positive for a partial sleeve, but only beats the risk-only engine at about 1.3× the current fee ratio. At 1.5× the 28% sleeve is worth roughly +264.
